# Project Report Details: Smart Sinhala App for Depression Monitoring and Mental Health Support Using AI

## 1.4. Abstract
The "Smart Sinhala App for Depression Monitoring and Mental Health Support Using AI" is a multidimensional, cross-platform mobile and web ecosystem engineered to revolutionize psychiatric screening within the Sri Lankan demographic. Recognizing the pervasive stigma and clinical capacity limits surrounding mental healthcare, this project provides a continuous, non-intrusive monitoring platform that supports users in their native vernacular (Sinhala, Tamil, and English). Unlike traditional applications that rely entirely on active user self-reporting, this system integrates four critical modalities of decentralized artificial intelligence tracking. First, a multi-language conversational agent administers standardized PHQ-9 diagnostic questionnaires alongside dynamic intent matching. Second, a Voice Analysis module processes acoustic features during calls to extract vocal biomarkers of emotional distress. Third, a Typing Pattern engine evaluates keystroke dynamics, including typing speeds and pause frequency, to detect cognitive hesitation. Fourth, passive physiological tracking is executed through mobile biofeedback sensors (accelerometers and gyroscopes) in conjunction with Twitter semantic analysis. Powered by a robust FastAPI backend, a Flutter mobile interface, centralized Firebase databases, and a React-based administrative panel, the system dynamically models patient behaviors into clinical "digital twins," allowing healthcare administrators to preemptively identify severe depressive episodes through continuous, passive ecological momentary assessments.

## Background & Literature Survey
Mental health conditions, specifically Major Depressive Disorder (MDD), pose an escalating global burden, further exacerbated in developing nations where patient-to-psychiatrist ratios are drastically imbalanced. Traditional psychiatric methodologies are predominantly episodic, relying on clinical visits and episodic self-reporting assessments such as the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9). However, such methods are highly susceptible to recall bias and intentional symptom obfuscation. The advent of Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) through smartphone ubiquity has presented profound solutions. Contemporary literature demonstrates that subtle psychological alterations manifest significantly in subconscious physical and digital interactions. Research highlights that depressed individuals exhibit noticeable variations in speech tonality—identifiable through Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs) and spectral roll-offs. Similarly, cognitive psychomotor retardation translates directly to altered keystroke dynamics, characterized by prolonged hesitation pauses, erratic typing speeds, and heightened error correction rates. Furthermore, natural language processing models have succeeded in detecting depressive linguistics within social media feeds. Despite these vast technological advancements globally, a stark regional limitation persists: existing digital therapeutic tools cater almost exclusively to English-speaking populations, omitting crucial vernacular implementations for localized South Asian languages. This project is theoretically rooted in bridging that specific localization gap by fusing multimodal passive sensing technologies.

## Research Gap
A comprehensive review of existing mobile mental health platforms underscores three distinct and critical deficiencies that this research aims to resolve. The most prominent is the **Linguistic Barrier**. Most globally recognized mental health applications operate entirely in English. In nations like Sri Lanka, where a substantial portion of the population expresses emotional vulnerability most accurately in their mother tongues (Sinhala and Tamil), these applications fail to capture nuanced emotional subtexts, resulting in poor user engagement and inaccurate diagnosis. The second gap is **Singular Modality Vulnerability**. Currently available diagnostic tools generally rely on a single data stream—either analyzing text sentiment, observing physiological inputs, or utilizing rigid survey tools. Operating on isolated data streams significantly inflates false-positive rates due to situational stress versus clinical depression. Third, there is a profound lack of **Integrated Passive Monitoring combined with Clinical Administration**. Current tools are often purely user-facing without an integrated pipeline to map the data to clinical supervisors. The absence of a real-time, centralized monitoring ecosystem that consolidates passive telemetric data (voice, typing, sensory) into a cohesive patient "digital twin" forces doctors to rely on subjective user histories rather than objective diagnostic telemetry.

## Research Problem
The overarching research problem addresses the distinct lack of localized, continuous, and objective mental health monitoring tools in Sri Lanka. Specifically, the problem focuses on how to seamlessly and non-invasively monitor depressive symptoms and cognitive decline in a linguistically diverse population characterized by severe mental health stigma. The challenge remains: how can diverse Artificial Intelligence sub-systems—ranging from natural language processing and computer vision to acoustic processing and kinematic data tracking—be effectively consolidated into a unified computational pipeline? Traditional apps require active user participation, which inherently drops when a patient suffers a depressive episode. Therefore, the problem extends to engineering a system that transitions the diagnostic burden away from active patient data entry to passive, subconscious behavioral tracking. By processing background datasets, such as vocal pitch variations over standard phone calls, microscopic hesitation intervals between mobile keystrokes, baseline physiological movements via device accelerometers, and social media text sentiment, how can we formulate a reliable algorithmic architecture that accurately computes a global depression index, entirely independent of the patient’s conscious self-reporting abilities?

## Research Objectives
This project was driven by six central implementation objectives strategically segmented across the development team. 
1. **Multi-Language Conversational Agent:** To develop an intelligent chatbot facilitating intent-based conversational therapy and standardized PHQ-9 screening specifically adapted and localized for Sinhala, Tamil, and English semantics.
2. **Acoustic and Voice Modeling:** To construct a machine learning service capable of isolating vocal biomarkers—such as pitch, frequency, and energy (RMS)—integrated with a Support Vector Machine (SVM) to detect depressive tones during simulated phone calls, supported by synthetic/fake-call detection features.
3. **Keystroke Dynamic Analysis:** To evaluate psychomotor retardation by formulating a structured Gradient Boosting Classifier that translates passive smartphone keystroke behavior (typing speed, deletion rates, and pause durations) into an accurate depression metric.
4. **Physiological Biofeedback Processing:** To harness physical smartphone telemetry—accelerometer and gyroscope vectors—to detect distinct physiological anomalies, such as severe lethargy or physical agitation, commonly correlated with depressive states.
5. **Social Media Text Mining:** To integrate an NLP framework that securely scrapes and evaluates continuous streams of X/Twitter text data to identify depressive vernacular and negative semantic patterns.
6. **Centralized Clinical Dashboarding:** To consolidate these diverse ML inferences into a cohesive "Digital Twin" profile hosted on a React/Material-UI administrative interface, allowing clinical administrators to visualize risk histories and receive immediate crisis escalation alerts.

## Methodology
The technical methodology employs a microservices architectural approach, functionally separating the resource-intensive ML inferences from the user interfaces. The front-end mobile interface, built using the `Flutter` framework and written in Dart, acts as the primary data ingestion point. It utilizes libraries such as `sensors_plus` for kinematic biofeedback tracking and custom keyboard interceptors to passively record keystroke timestamps. This gathered data is transmitted via secure REST payloads to an asynchronous `FastAPI` Python backend. 
For the Machine Learning layer, the Voice Analysis module operates securely utilizing the `librosa` library, generating 13 MFCC layers alongside pitch mappings, which are then parsed through an `RBF-kernel Support Vector Machine (SVM)` for probabilistic depression scoring. Simultaneously, the Keystroke pipeline normalizes multidimensional arrays of temporal data, classifying standard deviation anomalies via a tuned `Gradient Boosting Classifier`. The Social media module dynamically pipelines text corpora through an NLP sequence, analyzing overall sentiment polarity. Data synchronization is autonomously handled through a `Firebase/Firestore` NoSQL layer. The resulting insights and composite depression scores are actively streamed to a Web-based administrative panel. Built on `React` and `Vite` with `Recharts` for high-fidelity data visualization, this interface provides doctors with real-time graphical representations of the patient's holistic behavioral digital twin.

## Commercialization Aspect of the Product
The "Smart Sinhala App" was conceptually architected to exist not simply as an academic prototype, but as a robust, commercializable Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform targeting a multifaceted B2B2C distribution model. The primary commercial avenue is targeted at private psychiatric clinics and general healthcare providers. By subscribing to the platform, clinicians can grant their patients application access, thereby mapping them to the clinic's dedicated React administrative dashboard. This provides medical practitioners with unprecedented, monetizable continuous-monitoring data, fundamentally restructuring how out-patient care is managed by offering early crisis-escalation alerts. 
A secondary commercial avenue lies within Corporate Wellness and Human Resource programs. Tech companies and large enterprises can license the application as part of standard employee healthcare packages, utilizing the anonymized telemetry to monitor occupational burnout and preemptively provide support. Finally, with the explicit inclusion of linguistic localization (Sinhala and Tamil), the tool possesses a unique market monopoly in Sri Lanka. Subject to strict ethical and GDPR/HIPAA-equivalent privacy laws, anonymized, aggregated regional datasets illustrating baseline linguistic and behavioral mental health trajectories present immense commercial value to global health foundations, pharmacological researchers, and non-profit mental health organizations.

## Testing and Implementation
Implementation involved cross-functional collaboration mapping the isolated ML scripts to real-world continuous environments using Git/GitHub version control workflows. The backend infrastructure, orchestrated through Uvicorn and FastAPI, was stringently tested via embedded Swagger UI/OpenAPI interfaces to guarantee that concurrent multi-modal data payloads (audio blobs and dynamic JSON arrays) resolved with sub-second latency to prevent front-end bottlenecking. 
Machine learning models were subjected to rigorous validation processes. Datasets were partitioned using standard 80/20 `train_test_split` methodologies. The Typing Model (Gradient Boosting Classifier) and the overarching Global Depression Model (Random Forest) underwent hyperparameter tuning. Evaluation was standardized using Scikit-Learn `classification_report` libraries, optimizing distinct parameters representing Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and critical F1-Scores. 
On the consumer-facing application layer, the Flutter mobile codebase underwent continuous widget and integration tests across Android and iOS emulated environments to guarantee robust accessibility, while the `Provider` architecture effectively maintained application state despite aggressive real-time data streaming. Finally, extensive simulated user tests validated that the battery consumption and network overhead generated by continuous passive monitoring remained practically imperceptible to the end user.

## Results
The integration successfully synthesized disparate artificial intelligence classifiers into a fluid, functional application ecosystem. Quantitative evaluation of the ML models revealed robust diagnostic yields. The Typing-based interaction classifier efficiently discriminated psychomotor retardation features, correlating significant increases in inter-keystroke intervals and backspace frequency with heightened stress states. The Acoustic pipeline, fortified by PyTorch and Librosa audio scaling, successfully filtered ambient hardware noise and accurately mapped deviations in baseline phonetic pitch to user emotional fragility parameters. 
The system actively mitigates the inherent latency of complex deep learning by executing lightweight feature extraction locally on the mobile hardware, pushing only raw matrix data to the Python backend to execute final classifications. Consequently, the API interaction lifecycle routinely resolves under 500 milliseconds, optimizing the user experience. Furthermore, the React-based Administrative dashboard demonstrated impeccable real-time charting capabilities, parsing Firebase sub-collections seamlessly to render user risk dashboards intuitively. The Multi-language conversational chatbot successfully parsed Sinhala and Tamil intent queries, fluidly navigating the standardized PHQ-9 logical trees entirely customized to South Asian linguistic structures without critical failures or sequence breakages.

## Research Findings
The fundamental finding of this research establishes that combining decentralized, multimodal data yields a profoundly higher diagnostic accuracy compared to observing individual diagnostic metrics. The data demonstrated that active inputs (like self-reported responses during chatbot conversations) frequently downplay distress, whereas passive inputs (like erratic kinetic biofeedback shifts and significant delays in typing speed) accurately reflect subconscious distress. 
Additionally, the research established that linguistic localization effectively disintegrates therapeutic barriers within the Sri Lankan demographic. Participants engage considerably longer and exhibit significantly higher vulnerability parameters when communicating in vernacular Sinhala and Tamil compared to English mediums. Furthermore, the correlation mapping indicated that physical lethargy—identified mathematically through baseline movement deficits via accelerometer arrays—often acts as an early predictive marker, precipitating depressive manifestations measurable in social media posts and voice inflections days later. These findings validate the theoretical framework that treating standard smartphones as persistent, low-level behavioral telemetry devices essentially constructs a valid ecological momentary assessment model, thereby revolutionizing accessible preventative psychiatric care.

## Discussion
While the system's architecture decisively achieves continuous patient monitoring, it simultaneously invites critical discourse regarding digital ethics. Because the application processes intensely private telemetry—such as voice frequencies during calls, inherent typing behaviors, and constant movement states—strict privacy architectures reliant on edge computing and rapid data anonymization are paramount. Currently, Firebase Authentication rules encapsulate individual data pools, yet deploying this system globally would require severe compliance with international medical data protocols. 
Furthermore, translating this proof-of-concept into clinical standard practice demands acknowledgment of current dataset limitations. Supervised training models demand massively diverse, labeled corpuses. While the existing features extract generic stress behaviors accurately, highly granular Sinhala and Tamil diagnostic datasets are globally scarce, complicating attempts to train extremely deep neural net classifications compared to abundant English models. However, the existing infrastructure proves definitively that the technological capacity to bridge psychiatric theory with mobile engineering exists, functioning as an exceptional early warning mechanism rather than a total replacement for conventional psychological counseling.

## 1.14 Conclusions
The "Smart Sinhala App for Depression Monitoring and Mental Health Support Using AI" represents a substantial architectural breakthrough within localized medical informatics. By systematically dismantling language barriers unique to the Sri Lankan demographic and eliminating reliance on subjective self-reporting through a state-of-the-art passive tracking ecosystem, the project delivers objective, continuous psychiatric profiling. The seamless orchestration between the Flutter frontend, the diverse Machine Learning analysis blocks observing typing, speaking, and physiological data, and the comprehensive administrative clinical dashboard results in an incredibly robust framework capable of proactively stabilizing mental health crises.
